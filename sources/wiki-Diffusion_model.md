In [machine learning](./Machine_learning), **diffusion models**, also known as **diffusion-based generative models** or **score-based generative models**, are a class of [latent variable](./Latent_variable_model) [generative](./Generative_model) models. A diffusion model consists of two major components: the forward diffusion process, and the reverse sampling process. The goal of diffusion models is to learn a [diffusion process](./Diffusion_process) for a given dataset, such that the process can generate new elements that are distributed similarly as the original dataset. A diffusion model models data as generated by a diffusion process, whereby a new datum performs a [random walk with drift](./Wiener_process) through the space of all possible data.[[1]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-song-1) A trained diffusion model can be sampled in many ways, with different efficiency and quality.

 

There are various equivalent formalisms, including [Markov chains](./Markov_chain), denoising diffusion probabilistic models, noise conditioned score networks, and stochastic differential equations.[[2]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-2) They are typically trained using [variational inference](./Variational_Bayesian_methods).[[3]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-ho-3) The model responsible for denoising is typically called its "[backbone](./Diffusion_model#Choice_of_architecture)". The backbone may be of any kind, but they are typically [U-nets](./U-Net) or [transformers](./Transformer_(deep_learning_architecture)).

 

As of 2024[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Diffusion_model&action=edit), diffusion models are mainly used for [computer vision](./Computer_vision) tasks, including [image denoising](./Image_denoising), [inpainting](./Inpainting), [super-resolution](./Super-resolution), [image generation](./Text-to-image_model), and video generation. These typically involve training a neural network to sequentially [denoise](./Denoise) images blurred with [Gaussian noise](./Gaussian_noise).[[1]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-song-1)[[4]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-gu-4) The model is trained to reverse the process of adding noise to an image. After training to convergence, it can be used for image generation by starting with an image composed of random noise, and applying the network iteratively to denoise the image.

 

Diffusion-based image generators have seen widespread commercial interest, such as [Stable Diffusion](./Stable_Diffusion) and [DALL-E](./DALL-E). These models typically combine diffusion models with other models, such as text-encoders and cross-attention modules to allow text-conditioned generation.[[5]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-dalle2-5)

 

Other than computer vision, diffusion models have also found applications in [natural language processing](./Natural_language_processing)[[6]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-6) such as [text generation](./Natural_language_generation)[[7]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-7) and [summarization](./Automatic_summarization),[[8]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-8) sound generation,[[9]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-9) and [reinforcement learning](./Reinforcement_learning).[[10]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-10)[[11]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-11)

 

## Denoising diffusion model

 

### Non-equilibrium thermodynamics

 

Diffusion models were introduced in 2015 as a method to train a model that can sample from a highly complex probability distribution. They used techniques from [non-equilibrium thermodynamics](./Non-equilibrium_thermodynamics), especially [diffusion](./Diffusion).[[12]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-12)

 

Consider, for example, how one might model the distribution of all naturally occurring photos. Each image is a point in the space of all images, and the distribution of naturally occurring photos is a "cloud" in space, which, by repeatedly adding noise to the images, diffuses out to the rest of the image space, until the cloud becomes all but indistinguishable from a [Gaussian distribution](./Normal_distribution)       N   ( 0 , I )   {\displaystyle {\mathcal {N}}(0,I)}  ![{\displaystyle {\mathcal {N}}(0,I)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/00d56975d9da87432a3e9bdddf59aec1dd990bcb). A model that can approximately undo the diffusion can then be used to sample from the original distribution. This is studied in "non-equilibrium" thermodynamics, as the starting distribution is not in equilibrium, unlike the final distribution.

 

The equilibrium distribution is the Gaussian distribution       N   ( 0 , I )   {\displaystyle {\mathcal {N}}(0,I)}  ![{\displaystyle {\mathcal {N}}(0,I)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/00d56975d9da87432a3e9bdddf59aec1dd990bcb), with pdf     ρ ρ  ( x ) ∝ ∝   e  − −    1 2   ‖ ‖  x  ‖ ‖   2       {\displaystyle \rho (x)\propto e^{-{\frac {1}{2}}\|x\|^{2}}}  ![{\displaystyle \rho (x)\propto e^{-{\frac {1}{2}}\|x\|^{2}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/d97f99837e411ce05242074fd7084be4ffd24a58). This is just the [Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution](./Maxwell–Boltzmann_distribution) of particles in a potential well     V ( x ) =   1 2   ‖ ‖  x  ‖ ‖   2     {\displaystyle V(x)={\frac {1}{2}}\|x\|^{2}}  ![{\displaystyle V(x)={\frac {1}{2}}\|x\|^{2}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/7937bef94a9350a5a1a82470017b7317cea884f6) at temperature 1. The initial distribution, being very much out of equilibrium, would diffuse towards the equilibrium distribution, making biased random steps that are a sum of pure randomness (like a [Brownian walker](./Brownian_motion)) and gradient descent down the potential well. The randomness is necessary: if the particles were to undergo only gradient descent, then they will all fall to the origin, collapsing the distribution.

## Score-based generative model

 

Score-based generative model is another formulation of diffusion modelling. They are also called noise conditional score network (NCSN) or score-matching with Langevin dynamics (SMLD).[[15]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-15)[[16]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-:9-16)[[17]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-:1-17)[[18]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-18)

 

### Score matching

 

#### The idea of score functions

 

Consider the problem of image generation. Let     x   {\displaystyle x}  ![{\displaystyle x}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/87f9e315fd7e2ba406057a97300593c4802b53e4) represent an image, and let     q ( x )   {\displaystyle q(x)}  ![{\displaystyle q(x)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/c38bbafe34a043d284f19231b946a76c0a4b16b4) be the probability distribution over all possible images. If we have     q ( x )   {\displaystyle q(x)}  ![{\displaystyle q(x)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/c38bbafe34a043d284f19231b946a76c0a4b16b4) itself, then we can say for certain how likely a certain image is. However, this is intractable in general.

 

Most often, we are uninterested in knowing the absolute probability of a certain image. Instead, we are usually only interested in knowing how likely a certain image is compared to its immediate neighbors — e.g. how much more likely is an image of cat compared to some small variants of it? Is it more likely if the image contains two whiskers, or three, or with some Gaussian noise added?

 

Consequently, we are actually quite uninterested in     q ( x )   {\displaystyle q(x)}  ![{\displaystyle q(x)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/c38bbafe34a043d284f19231b946a76c0a4b16b4) itself, but rather,      ∇ ∇   x   ln ⁡ ⁡  q ( x )   {\displaystyle \nabla _{x}\ln q(x)}  ![{\displaystyle \nabla _{x}\ln q(x)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/4af93047d53da39336a81a2cf0bc6b211cc96ab0). This has two major effects:

 
- One, we no longer need to normalize     q ( x )   {\displaystyle q(x)}  ![{\displaystyle q(x)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/c38bbafe34a043d284f19231b946a76c0a4b16b4), but can use any        q ~ ~     ( x ) = C q ( x )   {\displaystyle {\tilde {q}}(x)=Cq(x)}  ![{\displaystyle {\tilde {q}}(x)=Cq(x)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/e0cb9a2697e3bfc69381357d2586568ba1d6f68a), where     C = ∫ ∫     q ~ ~     ( x ) d x > 0   {\displaystyle C=\int {\tilde {q}}(x)dx>0}  ![{\displaystyle C=\int {\tilde {q}}(x)dx>0}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/ea5a6cdba3dcb7186323c085e2b6dd560c69c3f2) is any unknown constant that is of no concern to us.

### Latent diffusion model (LDM)

 Main article: [Latent diffusion model](./Latent_diffusion_model) 

Since the diffusion model is a general method for modelling probability distributions, if one wants to model a distribution over images, one can first encode the images into a lower-dimensional space by an encoder, then use a diffusion model to model the distribution over encoded images. Then to generate an image, one can sample from the diffusion model, then use a decoder to decode it into an image.[[23]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-:2-23)

 

The encoder-decoder pair is most often a [variational autoencoder](./Variational_autoencoder) (VAE).

## Flow-based diffusion model

 

Abstractly speaking, the idea of diffusion model is to take an unknown probability distribution (the distribution of natural-looking images), then progressively convert it to a known probability distribution (standard Gaussian distribution), by building an absolutely continuous probability path connecting them. The probability path is in fact defined implicitly by the score function     ∇ ∇  ln ⁡ ⁡   p  t     {\displaystyle \nabla \ln p_{t}}  ![{\displaystyle \nabla \ln p_{t}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/c60d739be3204db5cd745f527fd9f52ab029a166).

 

In denoising diffusion models, the forward process adds noise, and the backward process removes noise. Both the forward and backward processes are [SDEs](./Stochastic_differential_equation), though the forward process is integrable in closed-form, so it can be done at no computational cost. The backward process is not integrable in closed-form, so it must be integrated step-by-step by standard SDE solvers, which can be very expensive. The probability path in diffusions model is defined through an [Itô process](./Itô_process) and one can retrieve the deterministic process by using the Probability ODE flow formulation.[[1]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-song-1)

 

In flow-based diffusion models, the forward process is a deterministic flow along a time-dependent vector field, and the backward process is also a deterministic flow along the same vector field, but going backwards. Both processes are solutions to [ODEs](./Ordinary_differential_equation). If the vector field is well-behaved, the ODE will also be well-behaved.

### Diffusion model

 

For generating images by DDPM, we need a neural network that takes a time     t   {\displaystyle t}  ![{\displaystyle t}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/65658b7b223af9e1acc877d848888ecdb4466560) and a noisy image      x  t     {\displaystyle x_{t}}  ![{\displaystyle x_{t}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f279a30bc8eabc788f3fe81c9cfb674e72e858db), and predicts a noise      ϵ ϵ   θ θ    (  x  t   , t )   {\displaystyle \epsilon _{\theta }(x_{t},t)}  ![{\displaystyle \epsilon _{\theta }(x_{t},t)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/ac1d100b70d76dbb0e1429000edc61529a2c1724) from it. Since predicting the noise is the same as predicting the denoised image, then subtracting it from      x  t     {\displaystyle x_{t}}  ![{\displaystyle x_{t}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f279a30bc8eabc788f3fe81c9cfb674e72e858db), denoising architectures tend to work well. For example, the [U-Net](./U-Net), which was found to be good for denoising images, is often used for denoising diffusion models that generate images.[[45]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-:3-45)

 

For DDPM, the underlying architecture ("backbone") does not have to be a U-Net. It just has to predict the noise somehow. For example, the diffusion transformer (DiT) uses a [Transformer](./Transformer_(deep_learning_architecture)) to predict the mean and diagonal covariance of the noise, given the textual conditioning and the partially denoised image. It is the same as standard U-Net-based denoising diffusion model, with a Transformer replacing the U-Net.[[46]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-46) [Mixture of experts](./Mixture_of_experts)-Transformer can also be applied.[[47]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-47)

 

DDPM can be used to model general data distributions, not just natural-looking images. For example, Human Motion Diffusion[[48]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-:4-48) models human motion trajectory by DDPM. Each human motion trajectory is a sequence of poses, represented by either joint rotations or positions. It uses a [Transformer](./Transformer_(deep_learning_architecture)) network to generate a less noisy trajectory out of a noisy one.

 

### Conditioning

 

The base diffusion model can only generate unconditionally from the whole distribution. For example, a diffusion model learned on [ImageNet](./ImageNet) would generate images that look like a random image from ImageNet. To generate images from just one category, one would need to impose the condition, and then sample from the conditional distribution. Whatever condition one wants to impose, one needs to first convert the conditioning into a vector of floating point numbers, then feed it into the underlying diffusion model neural network. However, one has freedom in choosing how to convert the conditioning into a vector.

 

Stable Diffusion, for example, imposes conditioning in the form of [cross-attention mechanism](./Attention_(machine_learning)), where the query is an intermediate representation of the image in the U-Net, and both key and value are the conditioning vectors. The conditioning can be selectively applied to only parts of an image, and new kinds of conditionings can be finetuned upon the base model, as used in ControlNet.[[49]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-49)

### Upscaling

 

As generating an image takes a long time, one can try to generate a small image by a base diffusion model, then upscale it by other models. Upscaling can be done by [GAN](./Generative_adversarial_network),[[53]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-53) [Transformer](./Transformer_(machine_learning_model)),[[54]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-54) or signal processing methods like [Lanczos resampling](./Lanczos_resampling).

 

Diffusion models themselves can be used to perform upscaling. Cascading diffusion model stacks multiple diffusion models one after another, in the style of [Progressive GAN](./StyleGAN#Progressive_GAN). The lowest level is a standard diffusion model that generate 32x32 image, then the image would be upscaled by a diffusion model specifically trained for upscaling, and the process repeats.[[45]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-:3-45)

## Examples

 

This section collects some notable diffusion models, and briefly describes their architecture.

 

### OpenAI

 Main articles: [DALL-E](./DALL-E) and [Sora (text-to-video model)](./Sora_(text-to-video_model)) 

The DALL-E series by OpenAI are text-conditional diffusion models of images.

 

The first version of DALL-E (2021) is not actually a diffusion model. Instead, it uses a Transformer architecture that autoregressively generates a sequence of tokens, which is then converted to an image by the decoder of a discrete VAE. Released with DALL-E was the CLIP classifier, which was used by DALL-E to rank generated images according to how close the image fits the text.

 

GLIDE (2022–03)[[55]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-55) is a 3.5-billion diffusion model, and a small version was released publicly.[[5]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-dalle2-5) Soon after, DALL-E 2 was released (2022–04).[[56]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-56) DALL-E 2 is a 3.5-billion cascaded diffusion model that generates images from text by "inverting the CLIP image encoder", the technique which they termed "unCLIP".

 

The unCLIP method contains 4 models: a CLIP image encoder, a CLIP text encoder, an image decoder, and a "prior" model (which can be a diffusion model, or an autoregressive model). During training, the prior model is trained to convert CLIP image encodings to CLIP text encodings. The image decoder is trained to convert CLIP image encodings back to images. During inference, a text is converted by the CLIP text encoder to a vector, then it is converted by the prior model to an image encoding, then it is converted by the image decoder to an image.

 

[Sora](./Sora_(text-to-video_model)) (2024–02) is a diffusion Transformer model (DiT).

 

### Lightricks

 Main article: [LTX (AI Model)](./LTX_(AI_Model)) 

LTX is a family of [open source](./Open_source) [artificial intelligence](./Artificial_intelligence) video [foundation models](./Foundation_model) developed by [Lightricks](./Lightricks), first released in November 2024.[[57]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-57) The latest models, LTX-2, create videos based on user [prompts](./Prompt_engineering), conditioned on text, images, video or audio - and capable of generating audio and visual in a unified way.[[58]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-58)[[59]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-59) They were preceded by LTX Video, which was released in 2024 as the company's first text-to-video model.

 

### Stability AI

 Main article: [Stable Diffusion](./Stable_Diffusion) 

[Stable Diffusion](./Stable_Diffusion) (2022–08), released by Stability AI, consists of a denoising latent diffusion model (860 million parameters), a VAE, and a text encoder. The denoising network is a U-Net, with cross-attention blocks to allow for conditional image generation.[[60]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-:02-60)[[23]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-:2-23)

 

Stable Diffusion 3 (2024–03)[[61]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-:6-61) changed the latent diffusion model from the UNet to a Transformer model, and so it is a DiT. It uses rectified flow.

 

Stable Video 4D (2024–07)[[62]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-62) is a latent diffusion model for videos of 3D objects.

 

### Google

 

Imagen (2022)[[63]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-63)[[64]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-64) uses a [T5-XXL language model](./T5_(language_model)) to encode the input text into an embedding vector. It is a cascaded diffusion model with three sub-models. The first step denoises a white noise to a 64×64 image, conditional on the embedding vector of the text. This model has 2B parameters. The second step upscales the image by 64×64→256×256, conditional on embedding. This model has 650M parameters. The third step is similar, upscaling by 256×256→1024×1024. This model has 400M parameters. The three denoising networks are all U-Nets.

 

Muse (2023–01)[[65]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-65) is not a diffusion model, but an encoder-only Transformer that is trained to predict masked image tokens from unmasked image tokens.

 

Imagen 2 (2023–12) is also diffusion-based. It can generate images based on a prompt that mixes images and text. No further information available.[[66]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-66) Imagen 3 (2024–05) is too. No further information available.[[67]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-67)

 

Veo (2024) generates videos by latent diffusion. The diffusion is conditioned on a vector that encodes both a text prompt and an image prompt.[[68]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-68)

 

### Meta

 

Make-A-Video (2022) is a text-to-video diffusion model.[[69]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-69)[[70]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-70)

 

CM3leon (2023) is not a diffusion model, but an autoregressive causally masked Transformer, with mostly the same architecture as [LLaMa](./Llama_(language_model))-2.[[71]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-71)[[72]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-72)

 [![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Transfusion_%282024%29_Fig_1%2C_architectural_diagram.png/330px-Transfusion_%282024%29_Fig_1%2C_architectural_diagram.png)](./File:Transfusion_(2024)_Fig_1,_architectural_diagram.png)Transfusion architectural diagram 

Transfusion (2024) is a Transformer that combines autoregressive text generation and denoising diffusion. Specifically, it generates text autoregressively (with causal masking), and generates images by denoising multiple times over image tokens (with all-to-all attention).[[73]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-73)

 

Movie Gen (2024) is a series of Diffusion Transformers operating on latent space and by flow matching.[[74]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-74)
