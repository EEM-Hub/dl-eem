### Denoising Diffusion Implicit Model (DDIM)

 

The original DDPM method for generating images is slow, since the forward diffusion process usually takes     T ∼ ∼  1000   {\displaystyle T\sim 1000}  ![{\displaystyle T\sim 1000}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/a0ac506b6132020f08e5333fb547089bf535ea11) to make the distribution of      x  T     {\displaystyle x_{T}}  ![{\displaystyle x_{T}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/591426ff2cb83d4938e4025bf2d126628e1123b5) to appear close to Gaussian. However this means the backward diffusion process also take 1000 steps. Unlike the forward diffusion process, which can skip steps as      x  t    |   x  0     {\displaystyle x_{t}|x_{0}}  ![{\displaystyle x_{t}|x_{0}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/a8f50baa0e37ba485884dc1947ae04911bc3b09b) is Gaussian for all     t ≥ ≥  1   {\displaystyle t\geq 1}  ![{\displaystyle t\geq 1}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/6946f8f50822de9e8e5e4c460ecbd4c05c644e15), the backward diffusion process does not allow skipping steps. For example, to sample      x  t − −  2    |   x  t − −  1   ∼ ∼    N   (  μ μ   θ θ    (  x  t − −  1   , t − −  1 ) ,  Σ Σ   θ θ    (  x  t − −  1   , t − −  1 ) )   {\displaystyle x_{t-2}|x_{t-1}\sim {\mathcal {N}}(\mu _{\theta }(x_{t-1},t-1),\Sigma _{\theta }(x_{t-1},t-1))}  ![{\displaystyle x_{t-2}|x_{t-1}\sim {\mathcal {N}}(\mu _{\theta }(x_{t-1},t-1),\Sigma _{\theta }(x_{t-1},t-1))}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/ed3447acde57c5a19ffbedb7e144bcff64fc1167) requires the model to first sample      x  t − −  1     {\displaystyle x_{t-1}}  ![{\displaystyle x_{t-1}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5218e6b1bb5ac7a2f8f61a01c8ab2cdce1a8ae1e). Attempting to directly sample      x  t − −  2    |   x  t     {\displaystyle x_{t-2}|x_{t}}  ![{\displaystyle x_{t-2}|x_{t}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/8eb5247b3f1445207d07bc4dd2c9e8becf9c49f5) would require us to marginalize out      x  t − −  1     {\displaystyle x_{t-1}}  ![{\displaystyle x_{t-1}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5218e6b1bb5ac7a2f8f61a01c8ab2cdce1a8ae1e), which is generally intractable.

In other words, the reverse process (and also the forward process) is deterministic. When using fewer sampling steps, DDIM outperforms DDPM.

### Latent diffusion model (LDM)

 Main article: [Latent diffusion model](./Latent_diffusion_model) 

Since the diffusion model is a general method for modelling probability distributions, if one wants to model a distribution over images, one can first encode the images into a lower-dimensional space by an encoder, then use a diffusion model to model the distribution over encoded images. Then to generate an image, one can sample from the diffusion model, then use a decoder to decode it into an image.[[23]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-:2-23)

 

The encoder-decoder pair is most often a [variational autoencoder](./Variational_autoencoder) (VAE).

### Classifier guidance

 

Classifier guidance was proposed in 2021 to improve class-conditional generation by using a classifier. The original publication used [CLIP text encoders](./Contrastive_Language-Image_Pre-training) to improve text-conditional image generation.[[27]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-:8-27)

 

Suppose we wish to sample not from the entire distribution of images, but conditional on the image description. We don't want to sample a generic image, but an image that fits the description "black cat with red eyes". Generally, we want to sample from the distribution     p ( x  |  y )   {\displaystyle p(x|y)}  ![{\displaystyle p(x|y)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b99b32ed9c0b94759956558b359f8da3ab98a23f), where     x   {\displaystyle x}  ![{\displaystyle x}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/87f9e315fd7e2ba406057a97300593c4802b53e4) ranges over images, and     y   {\displaystyle y}  ![{\displaystyle y}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b8a6208ec717213d4317e666f1ae872e00620a0d) ranges over classes of images (a description "black cat with red eyes" is just a very detailed class, and a class "cat" is just a very vague description).

 

Taking the perspective of the [noisy channel model](./Noisy_channel_model), we can understand the process as follows: To generate an image     x   {\displaystyle x}  ![{\displaystyle x}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/87f9e315fd7e2ba406057a97300593c4802b53e4) conditional on description     y   {\displaystyle y}  ![{\displaystyle y}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b8a6208ec717213d4317e666f1ae872e00620a0d), we imagine that the requester really had in mind an image     x   {\displaystyle x}  ![{\displaystyle x}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/87f9e315fd7e2ba406057a97300593c4802b53e4), but the image is passed through a noisy channel and came out garbled, as     y   {\displaystyle y}  ![{\displaystyle y}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b8a6208ec717213d4317e666f1ae872e00620a0d). ### Samplers

 

Given a diffusion model, one may regard it either as a continuous process, and sample from it by integrating a SDE, or one can regard it as a discrete process, and sample from it by iterating the discrete steps. The choice of the "**noise schedule**"      β β   t     {\displaystyle \beta _{t}}  ![{\displaystyle \beta _{t}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b801e8682d2f1345dbeaca1657eef676bd12ab4a) can also affect the quality of samples. A noise schedule is a function that sends a [natural number](./Natural_number) to a noise level:     t ↦ ↦   β β   t   ,  t ∈ ∈  { 1 , 2 , … …  } , β β  ∈ ∈  ( 0 , 1 )   {\displaystyle t\mapsto \beta _{t},\quad t\in \{1,2,\dots \},\beta \in (0,1)}  ![{\displaystyle t\mapsto \beta _{t},\quad t\in \{1,2,\dots \},\beta \in (0,1)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/7fb61a297a77456b54bdf3d15659a54408df6774)A noise schedule is more often specified by a map     t ↦ ↦   σ σ   t     {\displaystyle t\mapsto \sigma _{t}}  ![{\displaystyle t\mapsto \sigma _{t}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f185176ce18317a7245950d7d01794cb265754a5). The two definitions are equivalent, since      β β   t   = 1 − −     1 − −   σ σ   t   2     1 − −   σ σ   t − −  1   2        {\displaystyle \beta _{t}=1-{\frac {1-\sigma _{t}^{2}}{1-\sigma _{t-1}^{2}}}}  ![{\displaystyle \beta _{t}=1-{\frac {1-\sigma _{t}^{2}}{1-\sigma _{t-1}^{2}}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/bb61745ac9ded677cde9e59446d3f125625ed323).

 

In the DDPM perspective, one can use the DDPM itself (with noise), or DDIM (with adjustable amount of noise). The case where one adds noise is sometimes called ancestral sampling.[[32]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-32) One can interpolate between noise and no noise. The amount of noise is denoted     η η    {\displaystyle \eta }  ![{\displaystyle \eta }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/e4d701857cf5fbec133eebaf94deadf722537f64) ("eta value") in the DDIM paper, with     η η  = 0   {\displaystyle \eta =0}  ![{\displaystyle \eta =0}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/2dc20bdff327bfeab94c37936d4c1a05dfbd9784) denoting no noise (as in *deterministic* DDIM), and     η η  = 1   {\displaystyle \eta =1}  ![{\displaystyle \eta =1}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/9b65b387d2e1c91321cafa675da485eabb20ca82) denoting full noise (as in DDPM).

 

In the perspective of SDE, one can use any of the [numerical integration methods](./Numerical_methods_for_ordinary_differential_equations), such as [Euler–Maruyama method](./Euler–Maruyama_method), [Heun's method](./Heun's_method), [linear multistep methods](./Linear_multistep_method), etc. Just as in the discrete case, one can add an adjustable amount of noise during the integration.[[33]](./Diffusion_model#cite_note-33)
